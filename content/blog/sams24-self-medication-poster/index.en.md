We congratulate researcher **Mais Markabi**, a graduate of the first Scientific Research Camp, on presenting a scientific poster at the poster exhibition of the [24th International Conference of the Syrian American Medical Society (SAMS)](https://society.sams-usa.net/2026/02/24/sams-24th-international-conference-2026), hosted by the Faculty of Medicine at the University of Aleppo on 27–29 June 2026. She is the first and corresponding author of:

> Determinants, Patterns, and Perceived Safety of Self-Medication among Adults in Aleppo City, Syria

## From graduation project to a first research presentation

The research began as a graduation project by students of the Faculty of Pharmacy at the University of Aleppo, supervised by **Dr. Wassim Abdelwahed**. The work did not stop at the defence: Mais and her team developed it into a scientific poster presented to physicians and researchers at an international conference, their first research presentation.

It is not the first time we see Mais's name on research: she is one of the authors of the [paper published in Hybrid Advances](/en/blog/hap-scaffold-hybrid-advances/), the outcome of the research fellowship that followed the first camp.

## What did the study look at?

Self-medication is the use of medicines for self-diagnosed conditions, or continuing a previously prescribed drug without a current prescription. It can save time and money, but it can also lead to misuse, delayed diagnosis and serious complications.

The team studied the 3-month prevalence of self-medication among adults in Aleppo City, its determinants, the drugs most used, and how safe people think it is. They ran a cross-sectional survey of **999 adults** aged 18–85, from 27 March to 9 May 2025, using an Arabic questionnaire adapted from validated instruments, with informed consent and anonymity.

## Main findings

- **48%** of participants had used medicines without a prescription in the previous 3 months.
- Analgesics were the most used (84.6%), followed by flu and decongestant medicines, antipyretics (52.1%) and **antibiotics (38.1%)**.
- The most common reasons: headache (66.5%), cold and flu (64.4%) and body aches (45.2%).
- 74.8% read the drug leaflet, and 72.3% would recommend self-medication to others.
- The rate in Aleppo was lower than in the Damascus reference study (67.3%), and higher among women and adults aged 18–25.

## Why it matters

Using antibiotics without medical supervision raises the risk of **antimicrobial resistance**, which is already high in Syria. And 19.2% of self-medicating adults had a chronic disease, which increases the risk of drug-related complications. Local data like this is what decision-makers, pharmacists and physicians need to target awareness where it is needed.

## The team

Supervised by Dr. Wassim Abdelwahed, the research was carried out by Mais Markabi (first author), Alaa Buz Khalloof, Jinan Alammouri, Sara Kourany, Tasneem Maktabi and Zainab Shikh Dibs, of the Faculty of Pharmacy, University of Aleppo.

When the project was developed into a poster, **Eng. Mulham Fetna**, founder of Boundless, joined the team for data curation, formal analysis, software and visualization.
