The journal you publish in can completely change the value of your paper. So what do these codes mean?

## What does Q mean?

**Q** stands for **quartile**. Journals in each subject are ranked by their influence and then split into four quarters.

- **Q1:** the top 25% of journals in the field, the most influential.
- **Q2:** journals between 25% and 50%.
- **Q3:** journals between 50% and 75%, with medium influence in the field.
- **Q4:** the bottom quarter.

## How is it calculated?

It is based on how many citations the journal's articles receive over a set period. The exact measure depends on the database: SCImago uses the SJR indicator, Scopus uses CiteScore, and Clarivate's Journal Citation Reports use the Impact Factor. So the same journal can sit in different quartiles depending on the source, the subject category and the year.

## Is every journal in Scopus excellent?

Not always. Being indexed in Scopus doesn't mean a journal is top quality.

## Before you submit

Check the journal's ranking first, for example on [scimagojr.com](https://www.scimagojr.com). Choosing the right journal is half the battle.
